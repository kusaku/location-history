Location history

User guide

Open one or more Google Takeout Records JSON files, monthly semantic history files, or an on-device Timeline JSON export. Gzip, our delta-compressed sample, and timestamped GeoJSON points work too. A new import replaces the current history; files selected together are merged and exact duplicates removed.

Export your history as JSON

Current Timeline data is stored on individual devices. Export from the phone that holds the history you want to view. Menu names can vary by app version.

Android · system settings
  1. Open your phone’s Settings, then Location → Location services → Timeline.
  2. Tap Export Timeline data, then Continue.
  3. Choose a folder and tap Save. Wait for the export-complete notification.

This route starts in the phone’s Settings app. Google’s Android export instructions ↗

iPhone / iPad · Google Maps settings
  1. Open Google Maps, tap your profile picture, then Settings → Location & Privacy. Some versions call this Personal content.
  2. Tap Export Timeline data, then choose Save to Files in the share sheet.
  3. Choose a folder and tap Save. The exported file is named location-history.json.

Google’s iPhone / iPad export instructions ↗

Legacy · Google Takeout (deprecated)

This is the old route for cloud-stored Location History. For Timeline that has moved to your phone, use the device export above. Previously downloaded Takeout archives still work here.

  1. Open Google Takeout ↗, sign in to the relevant Google account, and choose Deselect all.
  2. If available, select Location History (Timeline) and choose JSON as its format.
  3. Choose Next step → Export once, select a ZIP archive, then Create export. Download the archive when Google says it is ready.
  4. Extract the ZIP. In its Location History folder, find Records.json, Location History.json, or monthly JSON files under Semantic Location History.

Transfer the exported JSON to the device where you use this site, then choose Open location history. Extract ZIP archives first. Select all the JSON files you want to merge together in the same import.

Place history

Click a hexagon to inspect its original cell, or choose Draw place area on the map in any visualization mode. Click corners and choose Finish area; clicking the first corner or double-clicking also finishes. Undo corner removes the last corner, and Escape cancels or closes. For keyboard drawing, focus the map, pan with arrow keys, press Space to add its centre, and Enter to finish.

Choose the current window or All dates. Hour, accuracy and jump filters still apply. Calendar follows the Date window by default: Day shows hourly bars, Week shows an hourly timetable, Month shows a day calendar, and longer ranges use a yearly activity grid. All dates uses the yearly view. While following Current window, the calendar arrows step the map window too. Choose a view manually to browse with the arrows or date/year field; Follow Date window restores synchronization. Shade by locations or time in the area. Hover or focus an interval for its totals; select a day to filter its dates, or an hour to set both the date and hour filters. Switch to Recorded stays or By day for sortable tables, with Previous/Next for longer results. Every matching stay and day is available. Time includes movement between consecutive locations in the area less than 15 minutes apart. Short gaps merge stays without adding time.

Filters and date windows

Exclude jumps removes both endpoints exceeding 1,000 km/h within six hours, including conflicting locations at the same timestamp. Longer recording gaps are not treated as jumps. This heuristic is computed once per import; filters never join tracks across removed records. Use Accuracy to filter known measurement error; observations with unknown accuracy stay included.

Day, Week, Month, Quarter and Year resize the current date window from its start. Quarter spans three calendar months. Backward and forward move the window by the selected calendar unit; a custom window moves by its duration. Play repeats forward steps until the end of the history. Dates and hours use UTC.

Select any histogram bar to use its UTC dates. The 64 bars span the entire history, so their date ranges depend on the imported history. Short histories have disabled intervals without dates. Use arrow keys to move between bars, then Enter or Space to select.

Visualization modes

Heatmap blends nearby locations. Hexagons groups them into cells that adapt to zoom. Points shows up to 20,000 representative locations in view. Journeys connects consecutive observations, breaking at gaps over six hours, excluded records, dateline crossings, or implausible speeds.

Density metrics

Measure density by records, distinct UTC days per cell, or estimated stationary time. Time uses recorded visit duration or stationary intervals of at most 30 minutes within 200 metres, clipped to the observation’s UTC day and chosen hours. It is an estimate, not a complete measure of time spent. Colour strength adapts to the 95th percentile of occupied cells in view; compare patterns, not absolute values between zoom levels. Spread changes the radius in screen pixels. Click a hexagon or point for details.

Privacy & performance

The app processes imported files locally and keeps history in memory. Clear or reload to remove it. Display and filter settings are saved in this browser; imported locations and the map’s position and zoom are not. Google Analytics and Yandex Metrica measure visits and interactions, including Yandex Session Replay. The map and history controls are marked to hide their content from Yandex replay. The selected map provider receives map requests for the area you view; Google and Yandex satellite maps also load their provider’s SDK. jsDelivr serves MapLibre, and Google Fonts serves the typefaces. Large history files are read in chunks, with a limit of five million combined observations. Available device memory also affects import capacity.

Base maps

Choose a view tab and provider in Map settings. Google offers roads, terrain, satellite and hybrid; Yandex offers streets, satellite and hybrid. Microsoft Azure offers roads, dark roads, satellite, hybrid and world-scale shaded relief. OpenFreeMap, OpenStreetMap and Esri imagery are also available. Satellite shows imagery; hybrid adds street labels. Google and Yandex satellite maps use a flat, north-up view. Availability, usage limits and provider terms apply. Switching maps keeps your imported history and filters.
